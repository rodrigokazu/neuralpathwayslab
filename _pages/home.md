<div style="margin-top: 50px; text-align:justify;">

I'm a Brazilian-born British computational genomics researcher and [MND Association](https://www.mndassociation.org/research) Non-Clinical Junior Fellow at the [Sheffield Institute for Translational Neuroscience (SITraN)](https://www.sheffield.ac.uk/sitran), University of Sheffield, with a joint appointment at the [Stanford Centre for Genomics and Personalized Medicine](https://snyderlab.stanford.edu) and membership in [Cambridge Neuroscience](https://neuroscience.cam.ac.uk/member/rodrigokazu/).

My background is biology: undergrad in Rio, then a master's on brain evolution in artiodactyls ([Frontiers in Neuroanatomy, 2014](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fnana.2014.00128/full); [PNAS, 2019](https://www.pnas.org/doi/full/10.1073/pnas.1716956116)), before a CNPq-funded PhD in Cybernetics at Reading where I built open-source toolboxes for neuronal signal analysis. My first postdoc, at the Active Touch Laboratory in Sheffield, produced [FootSim](https://www.cell.com/iscience/fulltext/S2589-0042(22)02147-2?): a model of tactile responses across the human foot sole, built to restore sensory feedback to prosthetic limb users. It won the INSIGNEO Institute prize and was published in iScience (2022).

After industry and a fellowship at the Wellcome Sanger Institute, I joined the Cooper-Knock lab at SITraN. The centrepiece is the largest ALS single-cell multiome dataset assembled so far: 788,330 nuclei from 70 donors, mapping the regulatory architecture of motor neuron degeneration. A WDR49+ astrocyte subpopulation that looks protective against the disease has been the most unexpected result. I've also built a 10x Xenium spatial transcriptomics pipeline covering ALS/MND spinal cord, glioblastoma, and spinal cord injury, using DOT-based deconvolution, spatial niche identification, and network controllability to place GWAS-enriched cell populations within glial niche architecture. A separate project on TDP-43 splicing dysregulation as a shared mechanism across ALS, FTD, and Alzheimer's disease is under review at Nature.

In October 2026 I became independent with an MND Association Non-Clinical Junior Fellowship, "Uncovering spatially-resolved master controller genes in ALS motor cortex and spinal cord". I now lead my own research programme at SITraN, using network controllability to find the master controller genes of cell-type gene regulatory networks and testing them in spatial data from ALS motor cortex and spinal cord.
