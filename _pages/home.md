<div style="margin-top: 50px; text-align:justify;">

I'm a Brazilian-born British computational genomics researcher and [MND Association](https://www.mndassociation.org/research) Non-Clinical Junior Fellow at the [Sheffield Institute for Translational Neuroscience (SITraN)](https://www.sheffield.ac.uk/sitran), University of Sheffield, with a joint appointment at the [Stanford Centre for Genomics and Personalized Medicine](https://snyderlab.stanford.edu) and a member of [Cambridge Neuroscience](https://neuroscience.cam.ac.uk/member/rodrigokazu/).

I trained in biology in Rio, with a master's on brain evolution in artiodactyls ([Frontiers in Neuroanatomy, 2014](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fnana.2014.00128/full) and [PNAS, 2019](https://www.pnas.org/doi/full/10.1073/pnas.1716956116)), then did a CNPq-funded PhD in Cybernetics at Reading. In Sheffield I built [FootSim](https://www.cell.com/iscience/fulltext/S2589-0042(22)02147-2?), a model of touch across the foot sole for prosthetic feedback that won the INSIGNEO Institute prize (iScience, 2022).

After industry and a fellowship at the Wellcome Sanger Institute, I joined the Cooper-Knock lab at SITraN to work on the largest ALS single-cell multiome dataset to date (788,330 nuclei, 70 donors), now in revision at Nature Neuroscience. The atlas points to a WDR49+ astrocyte population that may protect against ALS. I also built a 10x Xenium spatial transcriptomics pipeline for ALS spinal cord, glioblastoma and spinal cord injury. Nature has accepted a study of TDP-43 splicing across ALS, FTD and Alzheimer's disease that I co-authored with Eran Hornstein's group.

In October 2026 I started my own programme at SITraN with an MND Association fellowship, "Uncovering spatially-resolved master controller genes in ALS motor cortex and spinal cord". It uses network controllability to find the genes that steer each cell type and maps them in ALS tissue.
